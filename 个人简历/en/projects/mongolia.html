Overview

Built in UE5 for PICO 4, this virtual exhibition follows a narrative of felt tents, cradles and oases through Mongolian culture. Visitors begin with everyday objects in a yurt, move through a train and a contemporary gallery, and reach a space themed around ecological restoration in Kubuqi. Walking, handling objects, watching and listening connect cultural content with regional development.

Research: move from cultural symbols to lived settings

Early research covered customs, objects, cultural history and future development, with interviews exploring how younger visitors and digital media learners engage with exhibitions. Feedback called for clear routes, natural interaction and layered information, as well as the chance to enter a yurt as a living environment rather than only read text and view pictures.

The design places familiar symbols such as the yurt and morin khuur back into specific settings. Visitors first encounter objects and their surroundings, then receive relevant interpretation, before spatial transitions introduce social change and ecological themes.

Four spaces, one visitor journey

01

Origins · The yurt

Carpets, a wooden floor, a stove, milk-tea utensils and a morin khuur create a domestic setting. Object handling and proximity-based narration connect customs to things visitors can approach and inspect.

02

Transition · The train

Older seats, luggage, a narrow aisle and movement outside the windows evoke a journey. The carriage acts as a narrative transition into migration, exchange and change over time.

03

Contemporary life · The gallery

Portraits, sculptures and display panels surround a video wall. Six separate video entries provide access to material from The Children of the State, allowing visitors to choose their viewing order.

04

Future ecology · Kubuqi

Gobi rock formations, desert-restoration footage, photovoltaics and green-energy content connect the space to ecological restoration. Location-triggered narration relates interpretation to the visitor’s surroundings.

Asset production: preserve the identity of cultural objects

Asset production starts with structural modeling in 3ds Max, followed by detail work in ZBrush, low-poly preparation, UV unwrapping and texturing. High-resolution detail is baked onto assets for the real-time scene. The morin khuur emphasizes the relationship between its headstock, neck, resonator and strings, while the yurt is organized around its timber structure, furniture and everyday objects.

Clothing is shaped in Marvelous Designer, with folds and edge details refined in ZBrush. Silhouette, materials and relationships between objects are considered together so that their defining features remain readable at typical headset viewing distances.

Environment and lighting: let scene changes carry the story

Warm local lighting and skylight give the yurt a domestic atmosphere. Lower ambient light, aged materials and deep shadows shape the train transition. The contemporary gallery uses muted cool grays and spotlights to emphasize sculptures and film, while red-orange rock surfaces, distant light and screens guide movement through the ecological space. Each chapter has its own mood and focal points.

Interaction: connect physical actions with exhibition content

The project is implemented primarily in UE5 Blueprints, using VRExpansionPlugin, OpenXR and Enhanced Input to organize headset tracking, controllers and interaction input. Movement, object handling and display controls form a connected visitor flow.

01

Movement and turning

The left thumbstick drives forward and sideways movement relative to the headset direction, while the headset and controllers retain physical tracking. The right controller uses snap turning to keep virtual movement and physical actions understandable.

02

A shared gripping interface

A sphere trace near the hand finds interactive objects. Objects supporting VRGripInterface can be gripped and released through the same interface, reducing repeated interaction logic across similar assets.

03

Video selection and playback

Six entries each map to a media source. Buttons tell MediaPlayer to open and play the selected source, which is displayed through MediaTexture and a screen material, linking selection to playback and presentation.

04

Interfaces in space

Controller-based WidgetInteraction operates 3D UMG interfaces. Object narration and location triggers add context, bringing information forward as visitors look at or engage with the relevant subject.

My contribution and iteration

I contributed to research on culture, objects and ecology, as well as modeling, level organization and selected interactions, including the video wall, voice triggers and visitor flow. My focus was translating cultural content into objects, routes and feedback within the space.

In early trials, some visitors completed interactions without connecting them to the cultural content. This led to changes in content selection, narration pacing and trigger placement so that interpretation stayed closer to the object being encountered. Further observation is needed to assess whether visitors understand the links between chapters and can navigate with fewer prompts.
