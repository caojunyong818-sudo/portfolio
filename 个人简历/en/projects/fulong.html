Overview

Welcome to Fulong New Street is a cultural experience and casual management game project set in a historic Macao neighborhood, developed through a collaborative design practice between Harbin Institute of Technology and Macau University of Science and Technology. Players become an apprentice of the God of Wealth, exploring the street, helping run shops and collecting cultural clues across three narrative periods: Ming–Qing, Republican and modern times. The team translated field observations of buildings, shops and local stories into characters, levels and interfaces that reveal the neighborhood through play.

Field research: finding game ideas in the street

The team observed shop distribution, the scale of streets and alleys, building facades, visitor activity and local culture, recording spatial and behavioral details through photographs and activity maps. Research moved through observation, selection, organization and translation: documenting facts before choosing visual assets and story clues, then relating street elements to the narrative periods.

Qingping Theatre became a key cultural reference. Theatre tickets, posters, stamps and street signs informed the visual design and were translated into item descriptions, NPC dialogue and results screens, placing cultural information within exploration and task completion.

Game concept: running shops across three eras

The intended audience includes visitors to the neighborhood, casual players and people interested in hand-drawn art and cultural history. A fictional apprentice’s journey connects Ming–Qing, Republican and modern settings as three task stages. Shops, characters and events are organized around these periods so players encounter the context while playing.

Whiteboard discussions and low-fidelity prototypes helped the team organize the story, tasks and interfaces before producing art assets and integrating them into the engine. The atlas presents the overall three-period plan; the video primarily demonstrates the opening narrative, management interactions at Qingping Theatre and the results flow.

Play flow: exploration, management and collecting

01

Street exploration

Players browse shops in a side-view street, talk to NPCs and enter tasks. Short dialogue segments are coordinated with expressions, background changes and audio to make local stories part of an interactive sequence.

02

Shop management and feedback

Inside a shop, the view shifts to an isometric scene with quick tap-based management actions. Click feedback, customer states and accumulating coins communicate the results of each action. Small-scale shop restoration forms another progression strand in the overall design.

03

Period tasks and collections

The task panel groups progression into Ming–Qing, Republican and modern periods. Collections organize content into shops, characters, events and landmarks, turning discoveries into cultural records that players can revisit.

04

Ticket-inspired results

The Qingping Theatre results screen uses a ticket and stamp motif to present income, customers served and time spent on one card. Cultural references become part of the feedback rather than serving only as scenery.

Art and interface: translating the street into readable scenes

The art takes gray brick and red windows as environmental references, using clear outlines, flat colors and hard-edged shadows to distinguish buildings and characters. Street backgrounds, shop signs and props are organized as separate layers for scene assembly and interface integration within a consistent visual style.

Detailed character portraits support dialogue, while chibi figures serve the management scenes. The side-view main screen, bottom navigation, period-based task panel and grid collections establish an interface hierarchy. Shop icons against simple backgrounds help connect scene transitions.

UE5 Blueprints: connecting actions into a complete flow

The prototype uses UE5 Blueprints to connect input checks, coin updates, interface feedback and level results. The atlas shows Blueprint structures for control logic, character interfaces, management screens and results, with a focus on keeping actions, state changes and visual feedback aligned.

For frequent tapping, core checks and effect triggers were organized into reusable macros, with logic adjusted around object creation and cleanup.

My contribution: game programming and engine integration

As a member of the technical team, I focused on game programming and UE5 Blueprint implementation, integrating the team’s dialogue, characters and environment assets into the interactive prototype. My work centered on transitions between tasks and screens, and on connecting tapping, management feedback and results into a playable demonstration.

The team worked across story, art and technology: the story group provided cultural research and scripts, the art group established visual rules and produced assets, and the technical group integrated them in the engine and implemented interactions.
